# Dark Sky Basics

## Dark adaptation
Your eyes need about 30 minutes in the dark to reach full night sensitivity. Any white light, even a phone screen for a few seconds, resets most of that progress. Wait at least 20 minutes before hunting for faint galaxies and nebulae.

## Red light rules
At Larkfield Common observers may only use red lights, and the red LED must be dimmable. Torches brighter than 5 lumens are not allowed on the observing field. Cover car dashboards and charging indicators with red film or tape.

## Choosing a night
Aim for nights with a Moon below 25 percent illumination, or schedule faint-object work for after moonset. The club publishes a Bortle-scale rating for each site: Larkfield Common is rated Bortle 4, and Pennywell Ridge is rated Bortle 3. Lower numbers mean darker skies.

## Seeing and transparency
Seeing describes how steady the air is, and it decides how much planetary detail you can hold. Transparency describes how clear the air is, and it decides how faint a star or galaxy you can reach. A night can be good at one and poor at the other, so log both on a 1 to 5 scale in the observing logbook.
