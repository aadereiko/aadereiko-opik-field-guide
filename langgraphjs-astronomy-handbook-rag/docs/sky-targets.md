# Sky Targets for Beginners

## First-night targets
Start with the Moon, then Jupiter and Saturn if they are up, and the Pleiades star cluster. These are bright enough to show well from Larkfield Common and need no star charts. The Moon shows the most detail along the terminator, the line between lit and dark, so look there first.

## Messier Marathon
The club runs a Messier Marathon each March, aiming to see all 110 Messier objects in a single night. Only the dark skies of Pennywell Ridge are used for it. The record for the club is 94 objects, and finishing 100 or more earns the Marathon badge.

## Meteor showers
The club holds a watch night for the Perseids in August and the Geminids in December. Bring a reclining chair and a blanket, because you look at the whole sky rather than through a telescope. Expect to count about 1 meteor per minute at the peak from a dark site.

## Astrophotography with a phone
A phone can capture the Moon through an eyepiece, using the club's phone adapter that clips to the eyepiece. Hold exposure to 1 second or less for the Moon, and use a 10-second timer so touching the screen does not shake the image. Phone adapters can be borrowed for a week without a deposit.

## Logging observations
Record every session in the club logbook: date, site, seeing, transparency, equipment and objects seen. Logbook entries count toward the Observer Award, which needs 50 logged objects. Logs can be handed in on paper or sent through the members' form.
